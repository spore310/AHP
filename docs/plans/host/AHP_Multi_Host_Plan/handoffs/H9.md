## Review (H9-11, PF-09)

An independent, read-only reviewer inspected the integrated tree against the
contracts. It found no authorization bypass and confirmed that every
PostgreSQL session-scoped write is behind the epoch fence and the owner-filtered
session lock, that adoption requires a free lease and no live member, and that
proofs are checked for every binding before the signature and replay record.

| # | Finding | Severity | Disposition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A revocation observed before the lease existed was never acknowledged (PostgreSQL) | High | Fixed. The baseline is taken at activation. |
| 2 | Order-gate overflow left a rejected confirmation unhandled | High | Fixed. Covered by `order.test.ts`. |
| 3 | The recovery refactor turned one `continue` into an early return, a single-host regression | High | Fixed by restoring the original control flow. A dedicated test was added afterwards; see below. |
| 4 | Remote subscriptions made inside `initialize` and root `subscribe` had no reply hold | High | Fixed. Replies pass; later notifications and ordering confirmations wait until the caller's reply is enqueued. |
| 5 | Transfer tore the session down before validating the target, did not recover from every failure, and bypassed the recovery serializer | Medium-high | Fixed. The target is validated first, any failure after the withdrawal restores the session, and one serializer in the session factory covers every caller. A session whose type cannot be restored is still suspended if the durable move fails after the withdrawal. |
| 6 | A failed restoration was never retried | Medium | Fixed. A distributed host reconciles every two seconds while a restoration is pending. |
| 7 | `host_events` was never pruned | Medium | Fixed. Retention defaults to 15 minutes. Covered by the PostgreSQL suite. |
| 8 | The merged root count was computed at arrival, not at release | Low-medium | Fixed. |
| 9 | The `serve` operation was not enforced | Low | Fixed. Required to start and to be a transfer target. |

the first review: the namespace guard, the adoption order, the retirement
and relocation exclusion, the shared work index, the Kubernetes example and
every new test. It found no authorization bypass and no split-brain path. It
confirmed the adoption try-lock uses the lease key, and that the recovery
control flow matches the original.

| # | Finding | Severity | Disposition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The namespace guard and `ahp_recreate_namespace` resolved tables through the caller's search path | Medium | Fixed. Both are pinned to their own schema. A probe calls them from a connection with another search path. |
| 2 | `TRUNCATE` and a plain `INSERT` bypassed the guard; the guard did not serialize with a joining host; recreation did not recheck that the store was idle | Low-medium | Fixed. Truncate and insert guards, the join lock, and the recheck were added. Truncate and insert were probed at once; the join lock and the recheck were probed after the third review asked for it. |
| 3 | Reconciliation restored sessions from a page read before an earlier restoration finished, so a session retired or moved in the meantime could be handed to the provider again | Medium | Fixed. Each session is read again immediately before it is restored, and one failing session no longer aborts the pass. |
| 4 | The replicated Deployment could not complete a rolling update, and the README misdescribed what Kubernetes reports | Medium | Fixed. `maxUnavailable: 100%`, an accurate README, and check K10, which performs the update on a cluster. |
| 5 | Adoption could wait on a dead owner's row while holding its own host row | Low-medium | Fixed. The row is taken with `NOWAIT`; a probe holds it and observes an immediate refusal. |
| 6 | A transfer held the host-wide write slot while provider handles were released | Low | Fixed. Only the withdrawal runs in the slot. |
| 7 | The H2-10 probe did not exercise the new adoption order, and read the deadlock counter before it could have been flushed | Medium, evidence | Fixed. A dead-owner adoption is probed against a held row and against a queued revocation; the counter is read after the flush interval. |

| 9 | The H8-09 probe did not contain the renewal regression | Low, evidence | Fixed. The renewal-after-lock-wait regression runs on one host while the probe asserts the other host committed writes. |

The reviewer also judged each new proof. Where it found one weak, the proof
was strengthened: the H3-04 outcome is now read from the store by a third
instance after the held write is released; the H5-06 revocation cases gained
variants in which the ingress is not told and the caller's socket stays open;
the H4-08 probe uses a handle that is still open; the H7-05 identity
comparison always runs. Writing the H5-06 variant exposed one more defect: an
ingress could answer a subscription whose owner link had already closed.
Fixed in the router and verified by removing the fix once.

Recorded and not changed:

- H8-07: an execution whose executor disconnected stays counted on its owner.
  This is the documented single-host contract, pinned by an existing test.
- H9-06: `deployment.test.ts` is a text check that keeps the manifests in
  step with the SDK. The validation is the cluster run.
- H7-09: the lost acknowledgement is injected with the error shape the
  PostgreSQL adapter uses, on the in-memory store.
- Two hosts that both lost their lease and membership, and adopt from each
  other in the same instant, are each refused at once: adoption never waits
  on another host's row.
